# Timeline View

The Timeline View places your scenes along a line of story time. Each scene sits at its story date: the date and time it happens in the story, not the day you wrote it.

## Turn on the Timeline

1. Open **Project Settings** and choose the **Timeline** tab.
2. Turn on **Enable Timeline view**.

This also turns on the Timeline fields in the sidebar: the story date, duration and end date. If you later turn those fields off in the **Metadata** tab, the Timeline view turns off too.

While the view is off, its tab is greyed out. Its tooltip says "The Timeline view is off. Turn it on in Project Settings → Timeline."

## Put a scene on the timeline

A scene appears on the timeline once it has a story date. Set it in the document's sidebar:

- **Story Date / Time:** a day, or a day and a time.
- **Duration:** a number and a unit: min, hr, day or yr.
- **End Date / Time:** choose **Override** to set an end instead of a duration.

A scene with a duration or an end date is drawn as a bar as long as the scene. A scene with only a date is drawn as a thin line with no label.

Scenes with no story date are left off the timeline. The count at the top left, such as "6 SCENES", counts only the dated ones. If no scene has a date yet, the view says "NO DATED SCENES".

## Open the Timeline

Choose the **Timeline** tab. A story date you set while the view is open appears straight away.

The timeline runs from the earliest dated scene to the latest, with a small margin at each end. Scroll sideways to see all of it.

## Zoom in and out

- Use the **−** and **+** buttons at the top right. The button between them shows the zoom, from 100% to 1000%. The view opens at 200%.
- Or hold Ctrl or Cmd and scroll. Scrolling on its own doesn't zoom.

When your scenes cover only a few days, zooming in adds the time of day to the date labels.

## Read the timeline

- **Rows:** scenes in a folder, such as an act or a chapter, share a row. The row's label shows the folder's name and how many scenes it holds. Scenes that sit directly in the top-level **Workspace** folder are in the last row, labeled "WORKSPACE".
- **Overlapping scenes:** two scenes in the same row whose times overlap are drawn one above the other, and the row grows taller.
- **Gaps:** when two scenes in a row are far apart in story time, a label between them shows the gap, such as "6h", "3d" or "26w". Gaps are shown in hours, days or weeks.
- **Names:** a scene's chip shows its name, cut short with "…" if it doesn't fit. A short scene's narrow chip shows only the first word of its name.
- **Point of view:** when scenes have a point-of-view character, each character gets their own color. With two or more characters, a key above the timeline pairs each color with a name. If some scenes have no point of view, the key also has an **Outline** entry for them.

## See a scene's details

Point at a scene to see a tooltip with its name and:

- **Date:** the story date, and the time if set.
- **POV:** the point-of-view character, when set.
- **Duration:** such as "3h", or "Point event" for a scene with no duration.
- **Source:** the folder the scene is in.
- **Notes:** the start of the scene's notes, when it has some.
- The scene's status, when it has one.

Click a scene to select it. Its details appear in the sidebar, and the Timeline stays open.

## Good to know

- Two scenes a few hours apart in the same row can be drawn on top of each other, although their times don't overlap.
- When your scenes cover a few days, a date label can appear twice in a row at lower zoom levels, such as "May 2, May 2".
