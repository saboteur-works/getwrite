## Overview

A writer who shares their projects on the home network (Feature 75) and has
paired their own devices (Feature 76) has, today, a paired device that can do
anything the desktop window can do over the network, including deleting a
project, locking and unlocking the encrypted workspace, turning encryption on,
exporting a plaintext copy, and permanently emptying a project's Trash. The
sharing copy admits part of this in an interim note. This feature gives the
writer five switches in the desktop app, one per kind of action, each on
(restricted) by default; makes the server refuse a limited action from a paired
device on its next request; ensures a paired device is never offered the
workspace passphrase prompt while unlocking is limited, because a server
refusal alone does not keep a typed passphrase off the unencrypted network;
and makes the paired browser honest about all of it, showing a limited control
disabled with the reason and making every refusal visible. The desktop window
is never limited. It also contains the two measurements the design rests on,
run before the dependent work, and one conditional fix to the encryption route.

## Goals

- The writer can switch each of five limits on or off in the desktop app only,
  every limit is on by default, and a missing or unreadable settings file means
  every limit is on.
- A request for a limited action from a paired device is refused by the server
  on its next request, with no restart, and the refusal is never the "not
  paired" response; the same request from the desktop window, or with sharing
  off, is never refused.
- While unlocking is limited, no passphrase prompt is offered on a paired
  device, including while the device does not yet know its own status.
- A paired browser shows each limited control disabled with the reason, tells
  the writer when a request is refused, and never shows "Project deleted" for a
  deletion that did not happen.
- The two prerequisite measurements are recorded and reported to the owner
  before dependent work, and the encryption route's `projectId` handling is
  fixed if, and only if, the measurement shows the flaw.

## Non-goals

- Switches for changing sharing, generating pairing codes, or listing,
  renaming and revoking devices. Those are desktop-only bridge calls with no
  network route and get no switch (product OQ-76; Owner decision, Gate 1,
  2026-10-10). Remote management is deferred, not decided against.
- A switch for creating the workspace passphrase from a paired device. It is
  always refused (FR-14).
- Per-device limits. The five settings are one set for all paired devices
  (product OQ-79). If per-device limits are added later they MUST NOT be stored
  in `device-credentials.json`.
- Limiting a paired device's deletion of a resource or folder to the project's
  Trash. That stays ordinary editing (Owner decision, 2026-10-09; product
  OQ-75).
- Any change to the gate's classification rules, the Host allowlist, the
  same-origin check, pairing codes, the device cookie, or encryption at rest.
- A fix for the `next dev` server under the desktop app listening beyond
  loopback with sharing off. It is recorded as context in FR-3 and tracked
  separately; the feature list has no entry for it (read, not run).
- Showing the limits in the desktop window anywhere except App Settings (Owner
  decision, Gate 2, 2026-10-10).
- The two-client save measurement and the last-save-wins statement (Features
  78 and 79), and the port change (Feature 80).
- TLS, headless or NAS delivery, the native Android app as a client, accounts,
  and per-person permissions.
- Fixing how a paired editor behaves when the desktop locks the workspace
  under it (OQ-14); FR-37 records it.

## User stories

- US-1: As a writer who shares my projects on my home network, I want to switch each of five limits on or off in the desktop app so that I decide what my paired devices may do.
- US-2: As a writer who shares my projects on my home network, I want to have every limit on by default, and on whenever the settings cannot be read, so that nothing is allowed by accident or after an upgrade.
- US-3: As a writer who shares my projects on my home network, I want to have a paired device refused for a limited action on its next request, and the desktop window never refused, so that my limits hold without a restart and without taking away what I can do at my desk.
- US-4: As a writer with an encrypted workspace, I want to have a paired device never offered the passphrase prompt while unlocking is limited, so that my workspace passphrase is not sent across an unencrypted network by mistake.
- US-5: As a writer using a paired device, I want to see a limited control shown disabled with the reason, and every refusal made visible, so that I am never left guessing why nothing happened or told a project was deleted when it was not.
- US-6: As a writer who shares my projects on my home network, I want to change the limits only in the desktop app, with nothing about sharing, pairing or devices reachable from a paired device, so that a paired device cannot loosen its own limits.
- US-7: As a writer deciding whether to allow unlocking from a paired device, I want to read a plain warning beside that switch about what switching it off means, so that I choose knowing the passphrase would cross the network.
